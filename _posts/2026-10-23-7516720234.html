Richard Hitchens peacefully passed away Monday 19th October. He was very respected and popular in our village of Randwick. He was a true Cornishman always proud of his roots and seeing the ships sail the Cornish Seas was everything to him! His passion mainly lied reading about Ships sailing the rough Seas since time began. He would devour books sometimes 4-6 in one week. Every morning the Cornish radio station would be on whilst having his porridge.
He adored moving to Randwick village with his wife Lorna many years ago. He cherished and loved our Church taking on the role of fixing and making complicated mechanisms to fit to his perfectionist standards. Richard was an extremely clever man, a warm and friendly man quite painfully shy inside but with a beaming smile that lite his face up from ear to ear to the point where his blue eyes sparkled. He loved to laugh and chat with all the residents and friends he came to know in the village and surrounding villages. Whilst holding down a wonderful career designing and drawing engines for flight travel he nursed his wife Lorna for over 40 years. He said he loved his job so much that he simply saw it as a hobby everyday. It came that easy to his very creative mind with drawing measurements and numbers down to the precise scale of accuracy. Then off his drawings went down for it to be made. I often thought to myself when I flew to Italy “Richard designed this plane engine so I’m sure it will be perfect”

He met his beloved wife when they were 16. Lorna was sat in The Flamenco Cafe above Bateman’s in Stroud. He stood by the Cafe door and saw her in the distance immediately saying to himself “I’m going to marry that girl” and sure enough he did. He was a man who knew where he was going in life and what he wanted in life. He adored his wife and his Gran. His Gran was his rock and spoke to her everyday until she passed. On his respite days he would jump in the car and zoom down to Cornwall with huge excitement to see his Gran. She was no ordinary Gran she was a lady of substance still dressed in Victorian bustle type dresses down to the floor her beautiful hair rolled in big huge curls and a Victorian bun. She was intelligent and very well read. She always had people delivering beautiful flowers, fresh veg and the poor butcher would have to deliver meat sprinting with it across the fields to her remote house. If the meat did not look fresh she would request he go back to the freezer van and fetch her a much nicer fresh piece.

She taught Richard etiquette and knowledge of society. Richard was always the gentleman opening doors for people. He often invited many friends to his house with the keenest of days being Friday for fish and chips. He was crazy about Fish and chips. It always reminded him of his true home ! His Career stretched to many years juggling this and being a Carer. He fully supported the bravery and courage of his wife Lorna having gone through 36 surgeries. Lorna whilst bed ridden accomplished her degree and belonged to Mensa. Richard was always immensely proud of Lorna. Together they had many laughs and shared their lives in their beautiful house tucked away up and above connnecting them to the Woodland. Deer families, Foxes and Rabbits would be regular visitors in their part woodland garden on the bank. Richard enjoyed all the seasons especially hot Summers sitting outside overlooking the magnificent views they had. He worked hard all his life for what they had and therefore enjoyed retirement and hobbies including playing snooker train travel reading and collecting historical facts about the Cornish Seas starting to piece then together his ancestral family tree. He told me many interesting breakthroughs he had discovered plus diaries kept by Great great Uncles travelling the Seas. To which brings me to how proud he was obtaining his Skippers Licence. Whenever he got chance he would drive to Cornwall and sail away into the distance. He was extremely generous to the church funding projects over the years. He was a kind and thoughtful man. I was privileged to have called him my good friend. We spoke many times about our dreams and aspirations; he adored the lighthouse he grew up next door to in Pendeen where nestled beside it a little row of cottages lay “Ennis Cottages” he was born there but in his heart he told me……he never actually left that beautiful cliff and high Seas! It was always there within him like an endless burning candle of light!

FG
